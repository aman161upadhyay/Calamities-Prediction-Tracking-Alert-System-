# Reader evidence and trust

Readers do not universally prefer human-written text. Porter and Machery's [2024 poetry study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-76900-1) found favorable blind ratings for generated poems and penalties from an AI-authorship label. Its first study involved 1,634 participants. Poetry preferences do not establish how engineers judge a technical article.

Marco, Gonzalo, and Fresno's [2025 study](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1304/) examines 1,471 stories with 101 annotators. Reader profiles help explain conflicting evaluations. Surface polish and expert judgments can differ. Test with the intended audience instead of relying on one model judge.

Doshi and Hauser's [2024 experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532/) analyzes 293 completed eight-sentence stories. AI ideas can improve individual ratings while reducing collective diversity. An editor should preserve Aman's supplied angle rather than average it into a generic one.

Specificity makes claims easier to inspect. Explicit uncertainty prevents a forecast from becoming a promise. These are craft and safety judgments, not universal causal findings about trust. A precise invented anecdote is worse than a less vivid truthful sentence. Ask readers to explain the claim and what remains uncertain.

Measure blind prose preference separately from a labeled disclosure condition. Never use blind preference to justify concealing assistance where disclosure is required.

The [Stanford HAI report](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-detectors-biased-against-non-native-english-writers) reports 61.22% false positives on the tested non-native TOEFL essay sample. This is a result for those detectors and that data, not a universal current rate. Vocabulary frequencies in Kobak and Liang are population measurements, not individual guilt scores.

Perplexity depends on the reference model and tokenizer. "Burstiness" has several definitions. Neither certifies human authorship. Record concrete factual errors and editing burden instead of a detector score. See [sources.md](../sources.md) for dates and evidence tiers.
